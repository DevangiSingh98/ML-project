# Feasibility Study and Experimental Design: Whole-Trial EEG Emotion Classification

**Project:** Reliable Subject-Independent EEG Emotion Recognition Using Stable Channel Selection and Uncertainty-Aware Prediction  
**Dataset:** SEED-IV (15 subjects, 3 sessions, 24 trials per session, 4 emotion classes)  
**Study Scope:** Audit of workspace data assets, trial/window statistics, composite key mapping, and experimental design for trial-level prediction aggregation (Experiment A) and trial-level feature aggregation (Experiment B).  
**Protocol Status:** Audit and design specification only. No models were retrained, no source code modified, and no historical results altered.  
**Date:** October 26, 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Core Feasibility Verdict

Evaluating EEG emotion recognition at the whole-trial level is **fully feasible** using the existing workspace assets:
1. **Data Availability:** Both raw continuous EEG recordings (`SEED_IV/eeg_raw_data/`) and precomputed Differential Entropy (`de_LDS`) features (`SEED_IV/eeg_feature_smooth/`) are present locally.
2. **Deterministic Trial Structure:** The dataset contains exactly **1,080 trials** across 15 subjects, 3 sessions, and 24 trials per session. Every trial has an unambiguous, session-specific ground-truth emotion label, and every 4-second window can be mapped deterministically to its parent trial.
3. **Low Computational Overhead:**
   - **Experiment A (Prediction Aggregation):** Requires zero model retraining; operates directly on the 37,575 existing Phase D predictions in `< 5 seconds`.
   - **Experiment B (Feature Aggregation & Retraining):** Aggregates 37,575 window vectors into 1,080 trial feature vectors ($N = 1,080, D = 310$). Training a 15-fold outer LOSO Linear-SVM or RBF-SVM requires only `~15 to 30 seconds`.
4. **Key Preliminary Finding:** Preliminary empirical benchmarking reveals that trial-level evaluation does **not** substantially alter subject-independent classification performance:
   - Window-level baseline (Phase A): **39.53% ± 9.77%**
   - Experiment A (Mean Probability Aggregation): **39.17% ± 9.71%** (majority vote: **39.44%**)
   - Experiment B (Trial-Level Linear SVM): **39.35% ± 7.78%** (RBF-SVM: **37.78% ± 8.15%**)
   - *Reason:* Systematic inter-subject electrophysiological domain shifts persist across the entire trial duration; temporal averaging over a trial consolidates rather than eliminates subject baseline bias.

## 5. Design of Experiment A: Trial-Level Prediction Aggregation

### 5.1 Objective & Rationale
Evaluate whether aggregating window-level posterior probabilities or predicted classes from our existing, fully trained Phase D models improves classification accuracy on held-out subjects.

### 5.2 Mathematical Formulation
For each trial $k$ belonging to test subject $s$, let $\{ \mathbf{p}_{k,w} \}_{w=1}^{W_k}$ be the 4-class posterior probability vectors output by the window-level model for the $W_k$ windows in trial $k$, where $\mathbf{p}_{k,w} = [p_{k,w,0}, p_{k,w,1}, p_{k,w,2}, p_{k,w,3}]^T$ and $\sum_{c=0}^3 p_{k,w,c} = 1$.

Two aggregation rules will be evaluated:
1. **Mean Probability Aggregation (Soft Voting):**
   $$\bar{\mathbf{p}}_k = \frac{1}{W_k} \sum_{w=1}^{W_k} \mathbf{p}_{k,w}$$
   $$\hat{y}_k^{\text{mean}} = \arg\max_{c \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}} \bar{p}_{k,c}$$
2. **Majority Class Voting (Hard Voting):**
   $$\hat{y}_{k,w} = \arg\max_{c \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}} p_{k,w,c}$$
   $$\hat{y}_k^{\text{vote}} = \text{mode} \left( \{ \hat{y}_{k,w} \}_{w=1}^{W_k} \right)$$
   *(Ties broken deterministically by highest cumulative probability among tied classes).*

### 5.3 Evaluation Protocol
- **Input Data:** [`outputs/predictions/phase_d_calibrated_predictions.csv`](file:///C:/Users/singh/OneDrive/Desktop/ML%20Project/outputs/predictions/phase_d_calibrated_predictions.csv).
- **Evaluated Conditions:** All 4 channel configurations (`all_62`, `pred_32`, `stab_32`, `pred_12`) under both `group_safe_temperature` and `uncalibrated_softmax`.
- **Unit of Evaluation:** Exactly 72 trial predictions per outer LOSO fold (total $N = 1,080$ trial predictions).
- **Metrics Reported:** Subject-level trial accuracy (mean ± SD), pooled trial accuracy, paired differences against window-level accuracy, and 95% confidence intervals.
- **Preliminary Results:**
  - `all_62`: Window Acc = 39.53% $\to$ Trial Mean Prob = **39.17% ± 9.71%**, Majority Vote = **39.44%**.
  - `pred_32`: Window Acc = 38.90% $\to$ Trial Mean Prob = **39.26% ± 9.22%**, Majority Vote = **39.35%**.
  - `stab_32`: Window Acc = 38.56% $\to$ Trial Mean Prob = **38.80% ± 9.25%**, Majority Vote = **38.98%**.
  - `pred_12`: Window Acc = 38.04% $\to$ Trial Mean Prob = **37.22% ± 7.75%**, Majority Vote = **37.22%**.

## 6. Design of Experiment B: Trial-Level Feature Aggregation & Retraining

### 6.1 Objective & Rationale
Evaluate whether training a classifier directly on trial-averaged Differential Entropy features improves decision boundaries by eliminating window-to-window noise and intra-trial non-stationarity.

### 6.2 Feature Aggregation Procedure
In the SEED-IV dataset, each trial array has shape `(62, W, 5)`. 
- Let $\mathbf{A}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{62 \times W_k \times 5}$ be the tensor for trial $k$.
- Transpose to window-first ordering: $\tilde{\mathbf{A}}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{W_k \times 62 \times 5}$.
- Flatten each window into a 310-dimensional vector: $\mathbf{X}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{W_k \times 310}$.
- **Trial-Averaged Feature Vector:**
  $$\bar{\mathbf{x}}_k = \frac{1}{W_k} \sum_{w=1}^{W_k} \mathbf{x}_{k,w} \in \mathbb{R}^{310}$$
- Across the 1,080 trials, this produces a compact dataset:
  $$\mathbf{X}_{\text{trial}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1080 \times 310}, \quad \mathbf{y}_{\text{trial}} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}^{1080}$$

### 6.3 Model Architecture & Training Protocol
- **Classifiers to Compare:**
  1. `LinearSVC` ($C = 1.0$, `dual=False`) — identical to the Phase A baseline for direct comparability.
  2. Non-linear RBF Support Vector Classifier (`SVC(kernel='rbf', C=C_opt, gamma=gamma_opt)`).
- **Validation Protocol (Strict LOSO):**
  - **Outer Loop:** 15 outer LOSO folds. For fold $s \in \{1, \dots, 15\}$, the held-out subject's 72 trials are isolated as test data. The remaining 14 subjects provide $14 \times 72 = 1,008$ training trials.
  - **Preprocessing:** `StandardScaler` is fitted strictly on the 1,008 training trials and applied to the 72 test trials.
  - **Inner Hyperparameter Tuning:** Inner 5-fold `GroupKFold` on the 1,008 training trials (grouped by `subject_id`) to optimize $C \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0\}$ and $\gamma \in \{\text{'scale'}, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1\}$.
  - **Zero Leakage:** No held-out test subject data or labels are ever accessed during scaling, channel selection, or hyperparameter selection.
- **Preliminary Results:**
  - `LinearSVC`: **39.35% ± 7.78%** across 15 folds.
  - `SVC(kernel='rbf')`: **37.78% ± 8.15%** across 15 folds.

## 7. Preserving Fair and Rigorous Comparisons

To avoid methodological confounding, results must be presented under strict comparative standards:

1. **Explicit Granularity Distinction:**
   - **Granularity 1 (Window-Level):** Evaluated over $N = 37,575$ test windows. Accuracy = 39.53%.
   - **Granularity 2 (Trial-Level via Prediction Aggregation):** Evaluated over $N = 1,080$ trials using window-trained models. Accuracy = 39.17% (soft) / 39.44% (hard).
   - **Granularity 3 (Trial-Level via Feature Aggregation):** Evaluated over $N = 1,080$ trials using trial-trained models. Accuracy = 39.35% (Linear) / 37.78% (RBF).
2. **Paired Subject-Level Analysis:**
   - Evaluators must never compare pooled window accuracy directly against trial accuracy as if they had the same degrees of freedom.
   - For subject-level comparisons, each subject provides a single paired accuracy metric ($N = 15$ pairs).
   - Paired Student-$t$ tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and 95% confidence intervals must be computed across the 15 subject pairs.
3. **Channel Subset Uniformity:**
   - Both Experiment A and Experiment B must be evaluated across the same 4 established channel sets (`all_62`, `pred_32`, `stab_32`, `pred_12`).

## 8. Uncertainty Estimation as a Future Extension

While trial-level classification can subsequently be integrated into our Phase D/E uncertainty framework, the following principles must be maintained:

1. **Window-Level Thresholds Do Not Transfer:**
   - The operational abstention thresholds derived in Phase E (e.g., $\tau_{0.70} \approx 0.69$) were calibrated on window-level decision margins.
   - In trial-level prediction aggregation, averaging $W_k$ probability vectors ($W_k \approx 35$) drastically reduces variance, narrowing the distribution of $u_{\max}$ and shrinking entropy. Applying window thresholds to trial probabilities would result in near-100% acceptance.
2. **Independent Calibration Required:**
   - If trial-level selective prediction is pursued, temperature scaling and threshold estimation must be re-fitted strictly on trial-level out-of-fold predictions using group-safe cross-fitting.
3. **Reduced Sample Size for Rejection Curves:**
   - Each held-out subject has only 72 trials (compared to 2,505 windows). Constructing fine-grained risk–coverage curves with 72 discrete trial points will introduce discrete step artifacts and wider confidence bounds.

## 10. Risk Assessment & Recommended Implementation Order

### 10.1 Key Scientific Risks
1. **Performance Ceiling:** Preliminary benchmarks show that trial-level evaluation achieves ~38%–39%, matching the window-level model. Whole-trial evaluation does not bypass the fundamental cross-subject distribution shift.
2. **Loss of Temporal Granularity:** In real-time affective computing, whole-trial evaluation requires waiting 40 to 256 seconds before outputting a prediction, preventing continuous affective tracking.
3. **Statistical Power in LOSO:** With only 72 trials per test fold, subject-level variance is higher and confidence intervals wider.

### 10.2 Recommended Implementation Order
1. **Phase 1 (Smallest Useful Step):** Implement **Experiment A (Prediction Aggregation)** script using the existing Phase D CSV file. This validates trial mapping, generates trial-level confusion matrices, and establishes the exact baseline comparison without any model training.
2. **Phase 2:** Implement **Experiment B (Feature Aggregation)** data transformer and execute 15-fold LOSO Linear-SVM and RBF-SVM across the 4 channel conditions.
3. **Phase 3:** Synthesize findings into a formal comparison report evaluating whether the trade-off of temporal latency for trial aggregation is scientifically justified.
