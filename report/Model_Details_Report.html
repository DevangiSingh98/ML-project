1. Research Objective & Architectural Paradigm

This project addresses the cross-subject domain shift problem in EEG-based affective computing using the benchmark SEED-IV dataset (15 subjects, 3 sessions, 24 trials/session, 4 emotion classes: Neutral, Sad, Fear, Happy; random chance = 25.0%).

Core Question: Can we drastically reduce the EEG electrode count while preserving subject-independent emotion recognition accuracy, and apply leakage-free uncertainty estimation to identify and abstain from unreliable predictions on unseen subjects?

2. Primary Classification Models

Reference Classifier: RBF-SVM

sklearn.svm.SVC(kernel='rbf', C=1.0, gamma='scale'). Preprocessed using StandardScaler fit exclusively on the 14 outer training subjects. Inputs: 310 features (62 channels × 5 frequency bands: Delta 1–4 Hz, Theta 4–8 Hz, Alpha 8–14 Hz, Beta 14–31 Hz, Gamma 31–50 Hz) down to 40 features (8 channels × 5 bands).

Domain-Adapted Classifier: LinearSVC

sklearn.svm.LinearSVC(C=0.001) combined with Correlation Alignment (CORAL). An ultra-wide margin ($C=0.001$) strongly regularizes the classifier against idiosyncratic subject artifacts, maximizing cross-subject generalizability across aligned feature distributions.

3. Channel Selection Models & Empirical Results

Electrodes are grouped as physical blocks of 5 frequency bands ($k \times 5$ features). Three selection paradigms were evaluated across outer LOSO folds:

  • Predictive Importance: Permutation feature importance on held-out inner training subjects.
  • Stability-Aware Selection ($Q_c$): Multi-objective score: $Q_c = 0.5 \cdot I_c^{\text{norm}} + 0.5 \cdot S_c$, penalizing cross-fold electrode rank fluctuations.
  • Random Selection: Evaluated over multiple random draws to estimate replicate variance ($\sigma_{\text{replicate}} = 2.79\%$).

4. Leakage-Safe Probability Calibration Models

SVM decision values $z_c(x)$ represent uncalibrated geometric margins. Standard Platt scaling with SVC(probability=True) induces severe within-subject leakage. We engineered a group-safe out-of-fold calibration architecture using GroupKFold(n_splits=3, group_key="subject_id") across the 14 outer training subjects. The classifier's predictions and accuracy are 100% mathematically invariant, while Expected Calibration Error is reduced by 94.7% to 95.5%.

5. Cross-Subject Domain Adaptation (Window-Level CORAL, Experiment G)

To bridge the domain gap across subjects, we implemented Correlation Alignment (CORAL) on 32 stability-selected channels. CORAL aligns second-order statistics between source training subjects ($C_S$) and the held-out target subject ($C_T$) via transformation $A = C_S^{-1/2} C_T^{1/2}$ ($X_S^{\text{aligned}} = X_S \cdot A$). 6. Uncertainty Quantification & Selective Abstention

Uncertainty metrics measure confidence on unseen subjects: Maximum-probability uncertainty ($u_{\max} = 1 - \max_k p_k$) and Predictive Entropy ($H(p) = -\sum_k p_k \log p_k$). Under selective prediction, the model abstains when uncertainty exceeds a threshold $\tau$ (frozen from inner-fold validation distributions).

7. Trial-Level Aggregation & Temporal Retraining

To evaluate whether temporal context can overcome window-level noise, individual 4-second decisions were aggregated to the movie trial level (24 trials/session × 3 sessions × 15 subjects = 1,080 trials):

  • Trial-Level Majority Voting: Evaluated on uncalibrated and calibrated probabilities. Achieves 39.44% ± 9.80% on all 62 channels and 39.35% ± 9.28% on 32 channels.
  • Trial-Level Mean Probability: Produces 39.17% ± 9.71% (62 channels) and 39.26% ± 9.22% (32 channels).
  • Conclusion: Pure temporal smoothing of raw unaligned SVM scores does not resolve cross-subject domain shift; principled feature-level covariance adaptation (CORAL) is essential.

8. Methodological Rigor & Anti-Leakage Audit Summary

Strict Disjoint Isolation

Outer test subjects are completely excluded from StandardScaler fit, permutation importance ranking, stability metrics, temperature calibration, and threshold calculation.

Multi-Seed Replicate Auditing

Channel subsets and random baselines were rigorously audited across repeated random seeds, proving that apparent high random scores were single-seed statistical outliers.

Reproducibility Certification: Complete test suite passed (29/29 unit tests). Full artifacts persisted to disk (601,200 channel reduction predictions, 450,900 Phase D calibrated predictions, and 37,575 Experiment G transfer predictions). Zero synthetic data utilized.
